Test 3 · four-stage authoring

Build first. Connect inside the app. Then evolve it.

Five fresh corporate-tenant apps, one per model/effort configuration. Every app receives the same four frozen prompts: virtual-data foundation; Office 365 Users connection within that existing managed app; virtual approvals, asset lifecycle, themes and preferences; then a changed approval threshold, revisions, cost centres and mobile refinement.

Business records and approvals are virtual/local. Only the signed-in user's profile is live and read-only. An existing draft must survive every change. The final assessment covers all four stages: twenty checks worth five points, with at least 85/100 and every critical check required. Three planned follow-ups are counted separately from corrective or clarification prompts.

Actual cumulative task credits are recorded after each stage, after guidance and at the end. Use the final reading as the total; do not add cumulative readings together. The profile is independently verified in the built app, including after reopening.

One observation per configuration does not establish repeatability. Private profile screenshots and connection identifiers are not published.

What this does not establish

Task consumption is not a universal model ranking.

  • Test 3 has one observation per configuration; Test 2 has two. Neither establishes a reliable variance estimate or a promised price. Order, caching, service load and internal builder routing remain possible influences.
  • Meeting the 85-point and critical-check gate is not full coverage or production readiness. Profile fault/Retry coverage remains unverified in Test 3 (CX14), with additional per-run preference/cost-centre gaps. Connection-error/no-fallback coverage remains unverified in Test 2 (BC09). Large-list pagination, real user authorization, concurrency and scale are not established by the small synthetic fixture.
  • The selected Cowork model is not proof that every internal app-building worker used that model.
  • A shared connection and a synthetic persona switch are not production authorization. Sequential stock checks do not establish concurrent safety.
  • End-to-end time includes review and evidence capture. Comparable final timing is unavailable for Test 1.
  • The tests differ in app complexity, data source and review method. Their cost differences do not isolate the price of a connector.
  • Credits here are observed task consumption, not a currency amount or tenant invoice. App runtime, connector, storage, orchestration and deployment costs are not all measured by this comparison.
