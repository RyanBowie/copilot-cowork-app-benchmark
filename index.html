Demo benchmark — indicative costs only

This is a small demonstration benchmark using synthetic apps in demo tenant(s), not a production-cost study.

Your prompts and use cases can produce substantially different costs. App complexity, tenant setup, model/reasoning settings and follow-up work also matter. Use these results only as a brief indication of what an app can cost to build, not a quote, guaranteed price or complete running cost.

The managed app builder is available in Copilot Cowork and Copilot Studio. These measurements use Cowork /cost credits only; Copilot Studio costs were not measured separately.

Test 3 · four-stage authoring

Build first. Connect inside the app. Then evolve it.

The original cohort contains five fresh demo apps, one per model/effort configuration. Every app receives the same four frozen prompts: virtual-data foundation; Office 365 Users connection within that existing managed app; virtual approvals, asset lifecycle, themes and preferences; then a changed approval threshold, revisions, cost centres and mobile refinement.

Business records and approvals are virtual/local. Only the signed-in user's profile is live and read-only. An existing draft must survive every change. The final assessment covers all four stages: twenty checks worth five points, with at least 85/100 and every critical check required. Three planned follow-ups are counted separately from corrective or clarification prompts.

Actual cumulative task credits are recorded after each stage, after guidance and at the end. Use the final reading as the total; do not add cumulative readings together. The profile is independently verified in the built app, including after reopening.

The additional completed observations give two measurements for each paired setting, with amended private-publication permission. The original five remain separate. These small samples do not establish reliable future pricing. Private profile screenshots and connection identifiers are not published.

What this does not establish

Task consumption is not a universal model ranking.

  • The original Test 3 cohort has one observation per configuration; original Test 2 has two. Additional completed observations are labelled separately, with matched-setting means where available. These small samples do not establish a reliable variance estimate or a promised price. Order, caching, service load and internal builder routing remain possible influences.
  • Meeting the 85-point and critical-check gate is not full coverage or production readiness. Profile fault/Retry coverage remains unverified in Test 3 (CX14), with additional per-run preference/cost-centre gaps. Connection-error/no-fallback coverage remains unverified in Test 2 (BC09). Large-list pagination, real user authorization, concurrency and scale are not established by the small synthetic fixture.
  • The selected Cowork model is not proof that every internal app-building worker used that model.
  • A shared connection and a synthetic persona switch are not production authorization. Sequential stock checks do not establish concurrent safety.
  • Build time excludes review and evidence-capture gaps, but includes waits within an authoring turn. Build timing was not captured for Test 1.
  • The tests differ in app complexity, data source and review method. Their cost differences do not isolate the price of a connector.
  • Credits here are observed task consumption, not a currency amount or tenant invoice. App runtime, connector, storage, orchestration and deployment costs are not all measured by this comparison.
